## What the statement says

Fix n >= 1 and a number field K. Let l be a prime and let iota be a field isomorphism from the algebraic closure of Q_l to C. The statement uses two sets.

1. The automorphic side. A cusp eigenform is a nonzero cusp form phi on GL_n(A_K) of some level (IsCuspForm) that is a Hecke eigenvector at almost all finite places. Two cusp eigenforms are equivalent when they have the same eigenvalues of T_{v,1}, ..., T_{v,n} at almost all finite places. By strong multiplicity one, a class is a cuspidal automorphic representation pi. A class is algebraic when it contains a form phi with these properties. phi is smooth in the archimedean directions. At each real place w, the Capelli operator det[E_{jk} + (u - k) delta_{jk}] of the right action of gl_n(R) acts on phi as the polynomial (u + l_1) ... (u + l_n) with l_1, ..., l_n integers. At each complex place the same holds separately for the holomorphic and the antiholomorphic generators. The coefficients of the Capelli polynomial generate the centre of U(gl_n), and its roots are the Harish-Chandra parameters shifted by (n-1)/2 up to integers. So the condition says that the infinitesimal character of pi_infinity lies in ((n-1)/2 + Z)^n at each place, in the appropriate sense at complex places. This is algebraic in the sense of Clozel (C-algebraic in the sense of Buzzard and Gee).
2. The Galois side. These are the continuous homomorphisms rho from Gal(K-bar/K) with the Krull topology to GL_n of the algebraic closure of Q_l, that are irreducible and unramified at almost all finite places, up to conjugation by GL_n. Unramified at v means trivial on the inertia subgroup of every valuation ring of K-bar above v.

The pair (phi, rho) matches when there is a finite set S of finite places with this property. For each v outside S, each eigensystem ev of phi at v, and each sigma in Gal(K-bar/K) that is an arithmetic Frobenius at v, the polynomial X^n + sum_{i=1}^n (-1)^i q_v^{i(i-1)/2} ev(i) X^{n-i} equals the image under iota of the characteristic polynomial of rho(sigma). Here sigma is an arithmetic Frobenius at v when it stabilises some valuation ring W of K-bar above v and acts on W as x -> x^{q_v} modulo the maximal ideal. So any element of the coset Frob_v I_W counts. The roots of this polynomial are q_v^{(n-1)/2} alpha_i, with alpha_i the unitary Satake parameters. These are the Satake parameters of pi_v ⊗ |det|^{(1-n)/2}.

The statement says: there is a map Phi from the algebraic classes to the Galois classes, with three properties. Phi is injective. Phi sends [phi] to [rho] if and only if phi and rho match. Each irreducible rho with finite image is Phi of some algebraic class.

The Frobenius is arithmetic. With the usual normalisation (geometric Frobenius, rec of Harris and Taylor, which sends a uniformiser to a geometric Frobenius), Clozel's rho_pi has characteristic polynomial of rho_pi(Frob_geom) with roots q_v^{(n-1)/2} alpha_i. The rho of this statement has these roots for rho(Frob_arith). So Phi(pi) is the contragredient of the usual rho_pi, which is rho_{pi^vee} ⊗ epsilon_l^{n-1}. Since pi -> pi^vee preserves algebraic cuspidal representations and dualising preserves the Galois side, the statement is equivalent to the same statement with the geometric Frobenius. For n = 1 the matching says rho(Frob_arith) = chi(varpi_v), which is class field theory with the arithmetic normalisation of Artin.

## Quantifiers

n >= 1 is a hypothesis. Without it the statement is false for n = 0 (Langlands/Fidelity/GlobalGL.lean). Then l and iota are universal, and Phi is existential. It may depend on l and iota. The set S in the matching condition is existential for each pair and does not depend on v. The eigensystem and the Frobenius element sigma are universal, so the match must hold for every Frobenius lift at every v outside S. For an irreducible rho, Chebotarev and Brauer-Nesbitt show that the characteristic polynomials at the Frobenius elements outside a finite set determine rho up to conjugation. So the "if" direction of the matching condition holds as soon as Phi(pi) matches pi, and Phi is unique (reciprocity_map_unique). Injectivity is automatic: two classes that match the same rho have the same Hecke polynomials at almost all places, so the same eigenvalues, so they are equal. The content of the statement is the existence of Phi(pi) for each algebraic pi and the surjectivity onto the finite-image classes.

Two facts that Mathlib does not yet prove are needed for the Lean meaning to be the mathematical one. The cuspidal condition needs an invariant probability measure on (A_K/K)^{n x n}, which exists because A_K/K is compact. The matching condition needs arithmetic Frobenius elements IsFrobAt v sigma to exist at almost all v. They exist (decomposition groups surject onto the Galois group of the residue field extension), but this is not proved for IsFrobAt. If they did not exist, every pair would match. Both facts are true, so the statement is not vacuous.

## Weaker or stronger than Clozel and Fontaine-Mazur

- Same: the direction from pi to rho is Clozel's conjecture, with the twist |det|^{(1-n)/2}, for all algebraic pi (not only regular), all l and all iota.
- Stronger: the target asks rho to be irreducible. So the statement contains the conjecture that rho_pi is irreducible for each algebraic cuspidal pi. This is open even for regular algebraic pi over CM fields, outside special cases.
- Weaker: the converse is only for rho with finite image. That part is the strong Artin conjecture for irreducible Artin representations of K. The statement does not say that every geometric rho (de Rham at l, unramified almost everywhere) is in the image, so it does not contain Fontaine-Mazur for infinite image. It also does not say that the image of Phi consists of geometric representations, and there is no condition at l (de Rham, Hodge-Tate weights from pi_infinity).
- Weaker: the local-global compatibility is only at almost all finite places, through characteristic polynomials of Frobenius. There is nothing at the ramified places, at the places above l, or at the infinite places (for example the sign of complex conjugation).
- Normalisation: arithmetic Frobenius with the q_v^{(n-1)/2} twist. This is equivalent to the usual geometric form through the contragredient, as above.
- The statement is not trivially true. It contains the strong Artin conjecture and the existence of Galois representations for Maass forms of eigenvalue 1/4 (twisted by |det|^{1/2}). It is not trivially false: for n = 1 it is the theorem of Weil and Serre together with class field theory.

## Comparison with the docstring

The translation agrees with the docstring, and the module docstring explains the weakening in detail. The docstring of the statement does not say four things. First, the target requires rho irreducible, so the statement also asserts the irreducibility of rho_pi, which is an open conjecture beyond Clozel. The module notes say this, but the docstring does not. Second, with the arithmetic Frobenius, Phi(pi) is the contragredient of the usual rho_pi; the docstring of AutomorphicToGalois says this, but the docstring of ReciprocityGL does not. Third, injectivity follows from the matching condition and the definition of the classes, so it is not extra content. Fourth, the Lean meaning depends on the existence of arithmetic Frobenius elements, which is not yet proved for IsFrobAt. I recommend adding this paragraph after the list of properties: "The target classes are irreducible, so the statement also says that the Galois representation of each algebraic cuspidal pi is irreducible. This is an open conjecture even for regular algebraic pi. The statement uses the arithmetic Frobenius, so Phi(pi) is the contragredient of the representation in the usual normalization with the geometric Frobenius; the two forms are equivalent. Injectivity follows from the matching condition, because the classes on the automorphic side are the classes of Hecke eigensystems at almost all places." There is also a stale sentence in the module docstring of Langlands/Statements/GlobalGL.lean. It says that the reciprocity conjecture "need the infinitesimal character at the infinite places, which the vocabulary here does not have". Langlands/Statements/Reciprocity.lean now states ReciprocityGL with the Capelli operators. I recommend this text in place of that bullet: "The reciprocity conjecture for GL_n and the construction of Galois representations from regular algebraic cuspidal representations are in Langlands.Statements.Reciprocity, which has the vocabulary for the infinitesimal character at the infinite places."
